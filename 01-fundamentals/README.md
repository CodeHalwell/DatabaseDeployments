# Part 1: Database Fundamentals

Welcome to the foundation of your database journey! This section covers the essential concepts you need to understand before diving into specific database technologies.

## Overview

Understanding database fundamentals is crucial for making informed decisions about data storage, retrieval, and management in your applications. Whether you're building a simple web app or a complex distributed system, these concepts form the backbone of your data architecture.

## What You'll Learn

- The core concept of databases and why they exist
- Different types of databases and their use cases
- How to choose the right database for your needs
- The role of databases in modern software development

## Sections

### 1. [What Are Databases?](./what-are-databases.md)
Learn the fundamental concept of databases, their history, and how they've evolved to meet modern computing needs.

**Key Topics:**
- Definition and purpose of databases
- History of databases
- Database vs. file systems
- ACID properties
- CAP theorem

### 2. [Database Types and Categories](./database-types.md)
Explore the different categories of databases and understand their characteristics.

**Key Topics:**
- Relational databases (SQL)
- Non-relational databases (NoSQL)
  - Document stores
  - Key-value stores
  - Wide-column stores
  - Graph databases
  - Time-series databases
- NewSQL databases
- In-memory databases
- Comparison matrix

### 3. [Why Databases Matter in Software Development](./why-databases-matter.md)
Understand the critical role databases play in application development and business operations.

**Key Topics:**
- Data persistence and reliability
- Concurrent access and transaction management
- Query optimization and performance
- Data integrity and consistency
- Scalability requirements
- Real-world case studies

### 4. [Choosing the Right Database](./choosing-database.md)
Learn how to evaluate and select the appropriate database for your specific use case.

**Key Topics:**
- Requirements analysis
- Data structure and access patterns
- Scalability needs
- Consistency vs. availability trade-offs
- Budget and operational considerations
- Decision tree and selection framework

## Prerequisites

To get the most out of this section, you should have:
- Basic understanding of computer systems
- Familiarity with data concepts (files, records, etc.)
- No specific programming knowledge required yet

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this section, you will be able to:
1. Explain what databases are and why they're essential
2. Distinguish between different database types
3. Identify appropriate use cases for each database category
4. Evaluate database options for a given scenario
5. Understand fundamental database concepts like ACID and CAP
